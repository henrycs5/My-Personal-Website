Photo Journal

Week 1: Cohort & Campus Connection

I was coming back to my dorm when I saw this amazing sunset down the street. It brought a warm feeling of relief that I'd made it through a week's worth of Summer Bridge. Yet without the building tours or the scavenger hunt, I would be lost in navigating campus. Exploring campus allowed me to discover new places. Even building small friendships with fellow peers made me more involved in our cohort community. When I first came, I felt like an outcast. But building these relationships, learning about new concepts, and mapping out the environment made me belong.

Week 2: The College Toolkit

Working on assignments can be frustrating, especially debugging code. It can take me hours to solve a problem, but this blue duck gives me encouragement. It listens to me when I'm thinking of ideas and ways to approach a problem. A tangible object that brings me comfort when things are a little rocky. I see myself as the duck, a reminder to practice self-care by taking breaks. To balance how much I work and use my free time. Nevertheless, the duck is always cheering me on.

Week 3: Career Exploration & Preparation

I've always been fond of birds; seeing geese every day on campus brings me so much joy. These sightings reminded me of a children's book called "The Ugly Duckling." I never thought far into the future about what career I wanted to pursue. Yes, my inspiration is in Game Development, but I always had imposter syndrome. However, just starting my resume, creating accounts for both LinkedIn and Handshake, and learning about all these groups in UW is breathtaking. I was the duck that saw themselves as someone who didn't belong. Before I know it, I would be the goose that reunites with the herd in the skies.

Week 4: Personal Reflection

The more I removed values, the harder it got to tell which core values mattered most to me. Yet, my preferences over another value do not make it worthless. I discovered that the foundations of my motivations rest on hope. Hope gave me courage in projects that challenge my well-being. Hope allowed me to build friendships and be grateful for where I am in this moment. When I lose hope, it becomes a dark world. I begin to self-doubt my capabilities and often procrastinate, which makes me feel worse. Even if it doesn't feel like it in the moment, I know I will get through this storm.
